**Abstract**

This work presents some inventory models that are fundamental to enterprise resource management and decision-making in supply chains. The introduction gives a general overview of supply chains, their components and how they work.

Next, several deterministic and probabilistic inventory models are presented, together with an explanation of under which circumstances one is preferable to another. Regarding deterministic models, the classic EOQ (Economic Order Quantity) models and their variants are covered, including lead times, quantity-based discounts, multiple items and storage-space constraints. Regarding probabilistic models, two types are covered: continuous and periodic, including practical examples such as multi-period models, of which the newsvendor (stock-reservation) model is considered the most important.

In addition, so-called perturbation methods are presented, which make it possible to adapt the models to be closer to reality, along with how to handle inspection errors and returns.

The study closes with an update on the scientific literature. Finally, general conclusions are presented along with recommendations for future research directions to refine these models in increasingly complex and dynamic contexts.
